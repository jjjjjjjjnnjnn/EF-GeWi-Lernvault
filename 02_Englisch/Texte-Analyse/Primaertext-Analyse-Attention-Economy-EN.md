## 1. Primärtext — 原创仿写学术正文（Englisch）

### The Attention Economy: What We Sell When We Scroll

> ⚠️ 原创仿写 Modelltext（仿 anglo-amerikanischen Broadsheet-Kommentar / op-ed 风格撰写，非真实出版物摘录；数据与机构均为虚构）

Every day, somewhere, a person unlocks a phone to check the time and looks up forty minutes later, having forgotten both the time and the errand. We are tempted to call this a failure of character. It is nothing of the sort. It is the successful operation of a market. Over two decades, engineers have built the most finely tuned machinery of persuasion in history and aimed it at one finite resource: whatever you happen to be looking at. My claim is deliberately uncomfortable. Attention is not simply another commodity that firms compete to capture; it is the raw material from which a self is assembled. An economy that trades in attention is not selling minutes. It is selling portions of a person, with that person's cheerful consent.

The economic logic is brutal in its clarity. Attention is the one resource that cannot be stockpiled: a minute not spent is gone for good, and no technology will ever mint another. Because the supply is fixed, the competition for it is a zero-sum game, and in such a game whoever hesitates loses everything. Hence the arms race of autoplay, infinite scroll and push notifications. ⚠️ fiktiv: A 2025 survey by the (fictional) Institute for Digital Wellbeing found that 16-to-24-year-olds spent an average of 4.7 hours a day on algorithmically curated feeds; 68 per cent reported having opened an app "without remembering why". Scarcity, not curiosity, is what makes this market so ferocious.

It is tempting to reply that the feed merely mirrors what we already want. Although the platforms insist that their recommendations are neutral reflections of preference, this defence quietly changes the verb: the algorithm does not reflect desire, it anticipates and then manufactures it. A mirror shows your face; a prediction engine shows a face it has calculated you cannot resist — usually an angrier one. Autonomy requires that a choice be genuinely mine. A choice that has been modelled in advance, priced, and delivered at the precise moment of my boredom is mine only in a bookkeeping sense.

Nor is the design innocent. The feed never sleeps; it hunts, it learns, it waits. Scroll, swipe, smile, sleep — the rhythm is deliberately frictionless, because friction is the only place where a decision could occur. Every glance is measured. Every pause is priced. Every hesitation is harvested. Remove the pause and you have not persuaded anybody; you have removed the moment in which persuasion could have been refused. Granted, nobody forces a thumb to move. But a choice architecture engineered to bypass choice is not neutral ground; it is a room with the door behind you.

The consequences leak far beyond the individual. Because indignation travels faster than nuance, the market rewards the most inflammatory version of a story, and a public square organised around outrage is a poor place to disagree — and who, exactly, profits from that? ⚠️ fiktiv: In the same fictional dataset, posts expressing moral outrage were shared on average 3.4 times more often than posts expressing uncertainty. It is a delicious irony that we call this stream a "feed", a word that once meant care and nourishment. What is fed here is not the reader.

Must we therefore smash the machines? Hardly. The honest answer is smaller and harder: attention, unlike money, cannot be earned back, so the only defence is to stop treating it as spare change. Admittedly, regulation matters — transparency about recommendation criteria and an end to surveillance-based advertising would alter the incentives overnight. Yet law arrives late, and a generation cannot wait for it. The first act of resistance is embarrassingly modest: to notice. If attention is the substance of which a life is made, then to hand it over unexamined is not a harmless habit. It is a quiet, daily transaction in which we sell the one thing we cannot buy back, to a buyer who knows what he is getting.

## 3. Rhetorik- und Sprachanalyse（修辞与语言剖析）

> **中文理解**：每条走 **P.E.E.**（Point 点名手段 → Evidence 精确引文 → Explain 效果）。下表「Klausur-Formulierung」先给**德语分析句**（课堂/笔记用语），再给**英语考试可抄句**（英语答卷用这句）。

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "the raw material from which a self is assembled" / "not selling minutes … selling portions of a person" | **Metapher**（Ökonomie-Wortfeld：commodity, market, stockpiled, priced, transaction） | 把抽象的注意力变成可切割、可出售的物体，读者立刻感到「被当成货物」的冒犯，These 因此有了画面感。 | 🇩🇪 *Die Metaphorik des Marktes überträgt die Logik des Warentauschs auf das Subjekt selbst.*<br>🇬🇧 *By using market imagery — "raw material", "portions of a person" — the author turns an abstract resource into something that can be cut up and sold, which makes his thesis immediately tangible.* |
| 2 | "It is not selling minutes. It is selling portions of a person." / "A mirror shows you your face; a prediction engine shows you …" | **Antithese**（mit Parallelismus） | 两句结构对称、语义相对，制造「看似/实是」的落差，读者被迫放弃原有理解，接受作者的重构。 | 🇩🇪 *Die Antithese zwingt den Leser, die gängige Deutung aufzugeben und die des Autors zu übernehmen.*<br>🇬🇧 *Through the antithesis of "mirror" and "prediction engine", the author dismantles the common defence that algorithms are neutral.* |
| 3 | "Must we therefore smash the machines?" / "and who, exactly, profits from that?" | **rhetorische Frage** | 第一句先替读者问出极端方案（砸机器），随即以 "Hardly." 驳回，显得公允不偏激；第二句把责任推向受益者，激发读者的被利用感。 | 🇩🇪 *Die rhetorische Frage führt ein Gegenargument ein, um es sogleich zu entkräften; das wirkt ausgewogen und glaubwürdig.*<br>🇬🇧 *The rhetorical question raises an extreme counter-position only to reject it, which makes the author appear balanced rather than technophobic.* |
| 4 | "with that person's cheerful consent" / "a delicious irony that we call this stream a 'feed', a word that once meant care … What is fed here is not the reader." | **Ironie**（+ Wortspiel feed = füttern / Feed） | 读者先笑后醒：越是「自愿」越显得荒诞；"feed" 的词义反差把「被喂养」翻转为「被吞食」，刺痛感最强。 | 🇩🇪 *Die Ironie demaskiert die Zustimmung als Teil des Problems, nicht als Rechtfertigung.*<br>🇬🇧 *The irony of the word "feed" exposes the gap between what the technology promises — care — and what it does — consumption.* |
| 5 | "the most finely tuned machinery of persuasion in human history" | **Hyperbel**（+ Superlativ） | 夸张到历史尺度，读者感到威胁的规模远超个人意志力，为后文「不是性格失败」辩护。 | 🇩🇪 *Die Hyperbel entlastet den Einzelnen und verlagert die Schuld auf die Struktur.*<br>🇬🇧 *The hyperbole exaggerates the scale of the machinery, thereby freeing the individual reader from blame.* |
| 6 | "The feed never sleeps; it hunts, it learns, it waits." | **Personifikation**（+ Tricholon） | 让算法成为有意志的猎手，读者从「使用者」变成「猎物」，被动感与不安感同时上升。 | 🇩🇪 *Die Personifikation macht den Algorithmus zum Jäger und den Nutzer zur Beute.*<br>🇬🇧 *By personifying the feed as a hunter, the author reverses the roles: the user is no longer a customer but the prey.* |
| 7 | "Scroll, swipe, smile, sleep" / "Every glance is measured. Every pause is priced. Every hesitation is harvested." | **Alliteration**（s-/p-Laut）+ **Anapher**（Every …） | 四连词模拟顺滑的操作节奏，读起来像被推着走；三句 Anapher 一句比一句重（看→停→犹豫），收束为 Steigerung，压迫感递增。 | 🇩🇪 *Alliteration und Anapher erzeugen einen Rhythmus, der die Reibungslosigkeit des Mediums hörbar macht.*<br>🇬🇧 *The anaphora of "Every …" builds a climax that turns ordinary behaviour into something systematically exploited.* |

| 9 | "The first act of resistance is embarrassingly modest: to notice." / "stop treating it as spare change" | **Appell**（impliziter Imperativ + Direktadressierung "you"/"we"） | 把宏大问题缩小为「你可做到的一件小事」，读者从无力感转为可行动，收尾有正面推力。 | 🇩🇪 *Der Appell am Schluss überführt die Analyse in eine Handlungsaufforderung und bindet den Leser ein.*<br>🇬🇧 *The final appeal translates the analysis into a doable action, leaving the reader with a sense of agency rather than resignation.* |
| 10 | "Although the platforms insist that their recommendations are neutral reflections of preference, …" / "Granted, nobody forces a thumb to move." / "Admittedly, regulation matters …" | **Konzessivsatz**（Einräumung） | 三处让步让作者显得理性、不极端，随后反打更有说服力（ argues against own interest → ethos）。 | 🇩🇪 *Der Konzessivsatz räumt die Gegenposition ein, um sie im nächsten Zug desto wirksamer zu entkräften.*<br>🇬🇧 *By conceding that "nobody forces a thumb to move", the author strengthens his credibility before turning the concession against the platforms.* |
| 11 | "it is a room with the door behind you" | **Paradoxon**（als Bild） | 画面荒诞却一语中的：门在身后 = 你以为在自由选择，其实退路已被设计掉。 | 🇩🇪 *Das Paradoxon verdichtet die These in einem einzigen, merkfähigen Bild.*<br>🇬🇧 *The paradox condenses the whole argument into one memorable image: a room whose exit is behind you.* |
| 12 | "modelled in advance, priced, and delivered" / "autoplay, infinite scroll and push notifications" | **Trikolon / Aufzählung**（+ Steigerung） | 三段式节奏整齐、易记，读者感觉论证严密、层层加码，说服力和节奏感同步上升。 | 🇩🇪 *Die dreigliedrige Aufzählung erzeugt Dichte und suggeriert Vollständigkeit.*<br>🇬🇧 *The tricolon gives the argument rhythm and creates the impression of an exhaustive, well-ordered case.* |

## 5. Klausur-Transfer

> **中文提示**：以下 3 题按 **Teil A（integriert Lesen+Schreiben）** 的 AFB 分层设计；德语指令后括号内给出 NRW 官方英语 Operator，答卷**用英语写**，指令语言为德语。

### Aufgabe 1（AFB II，12 BE）

**Aufgabe**：*Analysieren Sie, mit welchen sprachlichen und rhetorischen Mitteln der Verfasser seine These stützt, Aufmerksamkeit sei mehr als eine Ware. Gehen Sie auf mindestens drei Mittel ein und erläutern Sie deren Wirkung auf den Leser.*
（Operator: **analyse** / **explain** — Englisch: *Analyse the author's use of language and rhetorical devices …*）

**Erwartungshorizont（踩分点）**

1. These 正确复述：注意力 = 自我的原料，不只是商品（2 BE）。
2. 至少 3 个手段**点名 + 精确引证**（Metapher / Personifikation / Anapher-Alliteration / Ironie / Antithese）（各 2 BE，共 6 BE）。
3. 每个手段都给出**读者效果**（Wirkung），不能只罗列（2 BE）。
4. 指出 Konzessivsatz 的论辩功能（让步→反打，提升 Glaubwürdigkeit）（2 BE）。

### Aufgabe 2（AFB II，10 BE）

**Aufgabe**：*Untersuchen Sie, wie der Text das Spannungsverhältnis zwischen individueller Entscheidungsfreiheit und algorithmischer Steuerung darstellt. Belegen Sie Ihre Untersuchung mit Textstellen.*
（Operator: **untersuchen** / **in Beziehung setzen** — Englisch: *Examine how the text presents the tension between …*）

**Erwartungshorizont（踩分点）**

1. 识别两组对立概念：Wahl/Spiegel vs. Vorhersage/Herstellung（3 BE）。
2. 分析「摩擦 = 决策发生地」这一论证核心（Entscheidungsarchitektur）（3 BE）。
3. 引用 "Granted, nobody forces a thumb to move." 说明让步—反打结构（2 BE）。
4. 用 "room with the door behind you" 说明自由的形式性与实质的被设计（2 BE）。

### Aufgabe 3（AFB III，14 BE）

**Aufgabe**：*Der Verfasser schreibt: „The first act of resistance is embarrassingly modest: to notice.“ Nehmen Sie dazu Stellung, indem Sie erörtern, ob die Lösung des Problems eher in individueller Selbstdisziplin oder in staatlicher Regulierung von Empfehlungsalgorithmen liegt. Beziehen Sie mindestens ein Gegenargument ein.*
（Operator: **Stellung nehmen** / **erörtern** — Englisch: *Comment on / Discuss whether …*）

**Erwartungshorizont（踩分点）**

1. 明确表态（Position）并**点名判据**（Kriterium：Autonomie / Wirksamkeit / Verhältnismäßigkeit）（3 BE）。
2. 至少 2 个**经解释**的论点（nicht nur behaupten），含具体例证（4 BE）。
3. 至少 1 个 Gegenargument 并**权衡而非否定**（Abwägung）（4 BE）。
4. 结尾给出明确 Urteil（Final judgement）+ 不超过 1 句展望，不引入新论点（3 BE）。

### 中文应试提示：本题型 3 个失分点

1. **只 Point+Evidence，缺 Explain**：列出 "personification" 并引用后，必须写清「读者为何被这样影响」——缺效果分析 = 半分答案。
2. **统计当事实抄**：本文两处数字标 ⚠️ fiktiv，考场上应写 `the (fictional) survey suggests …` 或分析其**劝服功能**，而不是把它当作已证事实复述。
3. **AFB III 只站一边 / 无判据**：表态句必须带 Kriterium（`I judge this by the criterion of autonomy …`），且必须真权衡反方（`Admittedly … yet …`）；只喊口号、结尾无 Urteil 同样失分。
