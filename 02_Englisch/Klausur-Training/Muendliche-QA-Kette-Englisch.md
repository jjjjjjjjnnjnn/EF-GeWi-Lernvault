### 1.3 Ablauf & 准备纸的用法（中文）

**流程**：拿材料 → **15 min 单独准备**（允许手写笔记，通常是 A4 一张或 Karteikarte）→ **10 min Vortrag**（可看笔记，但**只能看关键词，不能念整句**）→ **5–10 min Prüfungsgespräch**（考官就你的陈述追问，并把它拉向**更大的专业关联**）→ 评分。

**准备纸的三条铁律**：
1. **只写关键词，不写整句**。写整句 = 你会低头念 = Darstellungsvermögen 直接降档。

3. **留出右下角写"预判追问"**：准备期最后 2 分钟写下考官**最可能问的 3 个问题**（本篇 §4 已替你写好），进场前扫一眼。

## 3. 10 分钟英语标准陈述稿（Vortrag）

> **中文**：以下为**说出来顺口**的口语稿。`|` = 停顿（换气的记号，不是标点）；**加粗** = 重音落在那个词上。语速按 110–130 words/min 计，10 分钟约需 1100–1300 words；本篇按 **≈1150 words** 写，留有余量。

### 3.1 Einleitung（These + 议程 + 材料切入）

Good morning, and thank you for the task. | The topic I was asked to prepare is usually summed up in one short sentence: **social media makes teenagers ill**. | In the next ten minutes I would like to show why that sentence is both right and seriously misleading. |

The material in front of me is a set of fictional survey figures on daily social media use among fourteen- to nineteen-year-olds, together with a short opinion piece. | Both point the same way: heavier use goes with lower self-reported well-being. | But — and this is my thesis — **a correlation is not yet a mechanism**. |

So my claim is this: social media does not harm mental health in one simple, uniform way. | It does so through a particular economic logic, namely the attention economy, which turns human attention into a tradable good. | And once we understand that logic, we can also see where we might intervene. |

I will go about this in three steps. | First, I will outline how the attention economy works. | Second, I will look at why adolescents are especially exposed to it. | Third, I will weigh up what can be done — by the platforms, by schools, and by users. |

Let me begin with the mechanism.

> **中文提示**：这段在考 `outline` + These 的清晰度（AFB I→II 的起步）。考官 90 秒之内就要知道你**要证明什么**。
> **超时压缩**：删掉第 2 段整段（材料描述），把 These 直接接在第 1 句后。
> ⏱️ **entfallbar**：第 2 段整段（材料描述可直接省掉，开口就抛 These）、第 4 段最后一句 *"Third, I will weigh up what can be done…"*

### 3.2 Hauptteil 1 — Wie die Aufmerksamkeitsökonomie funktioniert

So, what is the attention economy? | Put simply, it is a market in which attention is the scarce resource and therefore the commodity. | The content is free; what is sold is our time on the platform, packaged as data and passed on to advertisers. | That is why the old line — *if you are not paying, you are the product* — is more than a slogan. |

How does a platform maximise that time? | Two mechanisms matter most. | The first is the **recommendation algorithm**. | It does not select for truth, and it does not select for what is good for us; it selects for what keeps us watching. | And what keeps us watching is whatever triggers a strong emotion: outrage, envy, admiration, fear. | So the feed quietly learns that balanced content performs worse than content that provokes. |

The second mechanism is what designers call **variable reward**. | The feed is unpredictable by design: most swipes give you little, occasionally one delights you — a message from someone you like, a video that makes you laugh. | That irregularity is exactly what makes the behaviour hard to stop; it is, in substance, the slot-machine principle. |

Let me make this concrete. | ⏱️ Imagine a sixteen-year-old — let us call her Maya. | She opens an app to answer one message. | Because the feed never ends and because the next swipe might reward her, she is still there forty minutes later. | Nobody forced her; the design simply removed every natural stopping point — there is no last page, no end of an episode. | And my material agrees: average daily use rose from 78 minutes in 2016 to about 171 minutes in 2024. | That is two and a half hours a day, and not because a generation lost its willpower. |

> **中文提示**：这段在考 `analyse`——机制（device）+ 效果（effect）两件齐。考官要听的是**因果链条**，不是"刷手机有害"这种结论。
> **超时压缩**：两段机制各留一句定义；Maya 的例子**必须留**（这是 `illustrate`，删了就没有细节分）。
> ⏱️ **entfallbar**：第 1 段最后一句（"old line"那句）、第 3 段最后半句（"slot-machine principle"）、以及 Maya 段中标了 ⏱️ 的整段（仅在时间严重不足时删）。

### 3.3 Hauptteil 2 — Warum gerade Jugendliche

That brings me to my second point: why adolescents in particular? | After all, adults use the same platforms. |

There are, I would argue, three reasons. | First, **timing**. | The adolescent brain is still developing, especially the systems for impulse control and long-term planning: the capacity to say "not now" is not finished, while sensitivity to social reward already peaks. | Second, **the nature of the reward**. | For teenagers, peer recognition is not a side issue; it is central to how identity forms. | Likes turn social standing into a number, and a number is easy to compare — which makes rejection visible as never before. | Third, **displacement**. | Time spent scrolling is time not spent sleeping, moving, or talking to someone in the same room: heavy users in my survey sleep about fifty minutes less than light users, and poor sleep alone affects mood. |

Now I want to be careful here, because this is where I partly disagree with the opinion piece in my material. | Use is not all the same. | My second table separates types of use, and the picture changes: passive scrolling scores around **54**, while active messaging with close friends scores around **71**. | So it is not "social media" as such that does the damage — it is the passive, algorithm-driven, quantified form of it. | That distinction matters, because it tells us we need not abolish the technology; we have to change which part of it we use, and how. |

> **中文提示**：这段是**全场最高分段落**——它同时做了 `analyse`（三个原因）和 `evaluate`（用材料修正材料）。**两张表的反差必须说出来**。
> **超时压缩**：三个原因各压成一句；**54 与 71 这两个数字一个都不能删**（这是你的 Beleg）。
> ⏱️ **entfallbar**：第 2 段中关于 "peer recognition / identity" 的第二句、以及 "makes rejection visible" 一句。

### 3.4 Hauptteil 3 — Was daraus folgt（drei Ebenen）

So what follows from that? | Let me turn to my third point — what can actually be done — and look at three levels. |

At the level of **design**, the obvious demand is to make feeds less compulsive: chronological rather than purely algorithmic ordering, no infinite scroll by default, and honest friction such as a prompt after a set time. | Critics will call that paternalistic; my answer is that the current design is already a choice, just one made for the company rather than the user. |

At the level of **regulation**, the measures usually discussed are age verification and transparency about how recommendations work. | I am sceptical about age bans, because they are easy to circumvent and they push teenagers towards less safe corners of the internet. | Transparency seems more promising: if we can see what the algorithm optimises for, we can at least argue about it publicly. |

At the level of **education**, schools have a real role — not as a single warning assembly, but as digital literacy: how recommendations work, why a feed feels the way it does, what your own attention is worth. | ⏱️ Let me give one small example. | A class that spends a lesson tracking its own screen time usually discovers the same thing: the time went somewhere nobody consciously decided to send it. | That insight, arrived at rather than announced, lasts longer than any warning. |

> **中文提示**：这段在考 `evaluate` + `gestaltend entwerfen`（AFB III）。考官要看你**有没有权衡**，不是列清单。
> **超时压缩**：三个 level 各留 2 句；"Maya"式的课堂例子可删。
> ⏱️ **entfallbar**：第 3 段最后两句（课堂例子整段）。

### 3.5 Schluss（结论 + Fazit + 开放问题）

Let me sum up. | My starting question was whether social media makes young people ill; my answer is: not simply. | The harm comes from a particular economic arrangement — an attention economy that profits from keeping us engaged, and that meets adolescents exactly when self-control is still under construction and peer approval matters most. | The fictional data supports the correlation; but the more useful finding is the gap between passive and active use, because it suggests the outcome is not fixed. |

Where does that leave us? | I would not say teenagers should withdraw from social media altogether — they would lose real friendships and real information. | But I do think they should be able to **name** the mechanism working on them. | Understanding it does not make you immune; it turns a private weakness into a public, discussable problem. |

That leaves one question I cannot settle here, and I would be glad to discuss it: | if attention is the resource being extracted, should we treat it as a commodity at all — or rather as something closer to a commons? | Thank you.

> **中文提示**：结尾在考 `comment`——**必须有立场 + 一个开放问题**（开放问题 = 给考官递话头，Teil 2 就顺着它聊）。
> **超时压缩**：第 1 段压成两句；最后一句开放问题**必须留**。

## 4. 5 组考官深挖追问 & 反驳反套路话术

> **中文总纲**：Teil 2 每次回答都跑四步（引自姊妹篇，此处只做执行）：**Respond → Advance → Justify → Relaunch**。只做第一步 = 把谈话控制权交回考官 = 降档。
> 三种核心话术：**① Concede & re-accentuate**（先认后转）· **② Differentiate**（区分 A 与 B）· **③ Supply evidence**（补证据）。

### Gruppe ① — 材料 / 数据质疑（Datenkritik）

**Fragen（EN）**
1. *"Your figures show a correlation between screen time and low mood. How do you know the causality does not run the other way?"*
2. *"The data is self-reported. Isn't that a serious weakness?"*
3. *"These are fictional survey figures. Can a made-up dataset support a real argument?"*

**考官意图（Prüfungsabsicht）**：测 **数据素养 + 方法意识**——你能不能看出相关性≠因果、自述偏差、样本局限。属于 `examine / assess`。

**Antwortstrategie（EN，骨架 3–4 句）**
> *"That is a fair challenge, and I would concede the point: my data can only establish a correlation, not a direction of causation. | Reverse causality is genuinely plausible here — young people who already feel low may simply use their phones more. | What I would add, though, is that the second table gives us something better than a correlation: it separates types of use, and passive scrolling scores 54 while active messaging scores 71. | So the mechanism I propose — algorithm-driven, quantified, passive use — is not just 'more time', and that is what I would want tested in a proper longitudinal study."*

**中文说明**：先**承认**因果不成立（别硬顶），立刻**补第二张表**把话题从"多少"转到"哪种"，最后主动提出更合适的研究设计——这是加分动作。

**Konter-Taktik（考官刁难 → 英语整句应对）**
| 刁难 | 应对整句（可直接说） |
|---|---|
| *"That is far too one-sided."* | *"I take that point — let me re-accentuate: my claim is not that time online is harmful, but that one specific, algorithm-driven form of it is."* |
| *"Belegen Sie das."* / *"Prove it."* | *"The best evidence I have is the contrast in the second table: 54 versus 71 points, with comparable amounts of total time."* |
| *"Ist das nicht widersprüchlich?"* / *"Isn't that contradictory?"* | *"It looks contradictory only if we treat 'social media' as one thing; I would distinguish between the platform as infrastructure and the feed as a mechanism."* |
| *"Und die Gegenposition?"* | *"The strongest counter-position is that total screen time has risen while some well-being indicators have stayed flat — which is exactly why I focus on the type of use rather than the amount."* |

**中文复盘**：最容易翻车的是**被逼承认"数据不能证明因果"后就哑火**。记住——承认之后**必须立刻接"但是第二张表"**，否则等于自己拆了自己的稿子。

### Gruppe ② — 概念澄清与定义（Begriffsdefinition）

**Fragen（EN）**
1. *"You keep using the term 'attention economy'. How exactly do you define it?"*
2. *"Is 'addiction' the right word here, or are you using it loosely?"*
3. *"What do you mean by 'mental health' — are you talking about clinical illness or everyday well-being?"*

**考官意图（Prüfungsabsicht）**：测 **概念准度 + 术语自觉**——你能不能给工作定义，并说明**你在哪个意义上用这个词**。属于 `analyse / explain`。

**Antwortstrategie（EN）**
> *"Let me define it operationally, because the term is used very loosely. | By 'attention economy' I mean a market in which attention is the scarce resource, so platforms optimise for time spent and sell that time on to advertisers. | On the word 'addiction': I would actually avoid it — the clinical criteria involve withdrawal and serious functional impairment, and most heavy users do not meet them. | 'Compulsive use' is more precise, and it also happens to be more useful, because compulsion is something design can produce without any individual being ill."*

**中文说明**：被问定义时**不要背词典**——给**工作定义**（"for the purposes of my argument, X means…"），然后主动**把自己用得不准的词换掉**（addiction → compulsive use），这一下就显出 C1 的术语自觉。

**Konter-Taktik**
| 刁难 | 应对整句 |
|---|---|
| *"That definition is too narrow."* | *"It is deliberately narrow — for the purposes of my argument I need a definition I can test, and a broader one would make every claim unfalsifiable."* |
| *"You are just using a buzzword."* | *"I would not deny it is a fashionable term; that is why I reduce it to a testable claim — platforms optimise for time, not for truth."* |
| *"Isn't every medium an attention economy? Newspapers too?"* | *"To a degree, yes — and that is a fair continuity. What changes is the granularity: a newspaper could not measure and re-target every individual reader every second."* |
| *"Define 'harm' precisely."* | *"I would distinguish between clinical harm and what I'd call a steady erosion of attention and mood — my argument concerns the second, and I should not have implied the first."* |

**中文复盘**：最容易翻车的是**把 addiction 说死**。口试里主动降低用词强度（"I would avoid that word / more precisely…"）是高分动作，不是示弱。

### Gruppe ③ — 反例与极端情形（Gedankenexperiment）

**Fragen（EN）**
1. *"Think of a teenager who spends three hours a day online and is perfectly happy. Does your argument collapse?"*
2. *"What if we removed the algorithm entirely — chronological feed only? Would the problem disappear?"*
3. *"During lockdowns, social media was the only contact many teenagers had. Isn't it then a mental health benefit?"*

**考官意图（Prüfungsabsicht）**：测 **反例处理 + 思想实验**——你能不能守住一个**概率性命题**（probabilistic claim）而不是被单个反例打穿。属于 `evaluate / discuss`。

**Antwortstrategie（EN）**
> *"That case does not collapse my argument, because I am not making a deterministic claim. | My claim is probabilistic: the design raises the average risk, and it raises it more for some users than for others. | A happy heavy user is entirely compatible with that — she may be using the platform actively, for real relationships, which my second table scores at 71. | And on the lockdown point, I would fully concede it: in a situation of forced isolation, the same technology was protective. That is precisely why I argue against blunt age bans and for changing the mechanism instead."*

**中文说明**：把命题从"X 必然导致 Y"降级为"X 提高 Y 的平均风险"——**单个反例就打不穿你**。再顺势把 lockdown 这个反例**收编**为自己的论据（反对一刀切年龄禁令）。

**Konter-Taktik**
| 刁难 | 应对整句 |
|---|---|
| *"So your claim is unfalsifiable?"* | *"No — it makes a testable prediction: hold total time constant, vary the type of use, and well-being should move. That is exactly the 54-versus-71 contrast."* |
| *"A single counterexample is enough."* | *"Only against a universal claim, and I am not making one; I am making a claim about average risk under a specific design."* |
| *"Then the technology is neutral after all."* | *"The infrastructure is close to neutral; the incentive structure built on top of it is not. That is the distinction I would insist on."* |
| *"You are moving the goalposts."* | *"I hope I am sharpening them instead: what I have narrowed is the claim, from 'social media harms' to 'this mechanism raises risk'."* |

**中文复盘**：最容易翻车的是**为了守住立场而否认反例**。正确姿势是**先收编再限定**（concede & narrow），考官真正扣分的是"死不承认"。

### Gruppe ④ — 价值权衡（Güterabwägung）

**Fragen（EN）**
1. *"You want regulation. Where do you draw the line between protection and paternalism?"*
2. *"Free speech and free markets versus the protection of minors — which side wins for you?"*
3. *"Isn't it the parents' job, not the state's?"*

**考官意图（Prüfungsabsicht）**：测 **论证性评判（AFB III）**——你能不能把两个正当价值放在天平上，给出**可辩护的优先次序**，而不是喊口号。属于 `evaluate / comment`。

**Antwortstrategie（EN）**
> *"I would not claim either side simply wins, because both values are genuine. | What tips the balance for me is asymmetry: one party is a minor, and the other has vastly more information about how the system works. | So I would argue for the narrowest measure that still works — transparency about the optimisation goal and friction by default, rather than content regulation. | Parents certainly have the primary role; but no household can negotiate with a recommendation system one to one, which is why I see a role for the state after all."*

**中文说明**：价值权衡的标准动作是**先承认双方都正当**，再给一个**可命名的判准**（这里是 Informationsasymmetrie / 未成年），最后**只主张最窄的手段**（narrowest measure）——这三步一走，考官很难说你片面。

**Konter-Taktik**
| 刁难 | 应对整句 |
|---|---|
| *"Das ist aber zu einseitig."* | *"Let me re-accentuate: I am not asking for censorship, I am asking for transparency — the least intrusive measure on the list."* |
| *"That is paternalistic."* | *"It would be paternalistic to tell people what to see; requiring them to be told how they are being sorted is something else entirely."* |
| *"Und die Meinungsfreiheit?"* | *"Freedom of expression is not at stake in a ranking decision — nobody's post is removed, only the order changes."* |
| *"Who decides what 'good for us' means?"* | *"That is exactly why I do not want the state to define good content; I want the platform to disclose what it currently optimises for, and let the public argue."* |

**中文复盘**：最容易翻车的是**一上来就说"应该禁止"**。口试里主张**最窄手段 + 说明理由**，比主张强硬手段得分高得多。

### Gruppe ⑤ — 迁移与应用（Transfer in die Praxis）

**Fragen（EN）**
1. *"How would you actually teach this in a Q2 English classroom — concretely?"*
2. *"What would you personally change about your own use tomorrow?"*
3. *"Does any of this transfer to other domains — news consumption, or even how we study?"*

**考官意图（Prüfungsabsicht）**：测 **迁移能力 + 自我反思**——你能不能把分析落到**具体可执行的做法**，并看到**更大的专业关联**。属于 `comment / reflect`。

**Antwortstrategie（EN）**
> *"Concretely, I would not do a warning lesson; I would make the mechanism visible. | Students track their own screen time for a week, then separate passive scrolling from active messaging — and they almost always find the same thing: the passive share is larger than they assumed. | The second step is rhetorical rather than technical: have them analyse a platform's own interface language — 'for you', 'you might like' — and ask whose interest that phrasing serves. | That transfers directly, incidentally, to news consumption: the same ranking logic that rewards outrage in a feed also rewards it in a news app."*

**中文说明**：迁移题要给**可操作的两个步骤**（不是"应该加强教育"这种空话），并且**自己给出跨领域联想**（feed → news → 学习方式）——这就是考官要的**eigene Impulse**。

**Konter-Taktik**
| 刁难 | 应对整句 |
|---|---|
| *"That is naive — teenagers already know all this."* | *"They know the facts, and still lose the time — which suggests knowledge alone is not the lever; making the mechanism visible in their own data is."* |
| *"One lesson will change nothing."* | *"Agreed, and I should not have implied one lesson is enough; what I am describing is a unit that returns to the same data three times."* |
| *"Isn't that the school overstepping again?"* | *"Not if the question is analytical: analysing how a text addresses you is already part of the English curriculum — a feed is just another address."* |
| *"What would you change about yourself, honestly?"* | *"Honestly: notifications off, and the app off the home screen — which is embarrassingly simple, and which shows how much of this is design rather than discipline."* |

**中文复盘**：最容易翻车的是**回答停留在抽象层面**（"应该提高意识"）。迁移题必须出现**动词的具体宾语**：track / separate / analyse / turn off。

## 5. 防卡壳急救词库（Notfall-Redemittel）

> **中文**：以下全部为**英语表达 + 中文说明**，口试现场可直接整句照搬。**建议考前把每类的第 1、2 条背到条件反射**。

### 5.1 Zeit gewinnen（争取思考时间）

| 英语表达 | 中文 | 使用场景 |
|---|---|---|
| *"That is a good question — let me think for a second."* | 好问题，让我想一下 | 任何追问的开场，最安全 |
| *"Let me put that in context before I answer."* | 我先把它放到语境里再答 | 需要 3–5 秒组织时 |
| *"If I understand you correctly, you are asking whether…"* | 如果我没理解错，您问的是…… | **重述问题**（顺便确认自己没听错） |
| *"There are two ways of answering that."* | 这有两种答法 | 为自己争取分点时间 |
| *"Let me take that in two steps."* | 我分两步说 | 拖时间的同时显出结构感 |
| *"I want to be careful here, because the terms matter."* | 这里我想谨慎点，因为术语关键 | 被问定义时先稳住 |

### 5.2 Umformulieren / Vereinfachen（换说法、降难度）

| 英语表达 | 中文 | 使用场景 |
|---|---|---|
| *"To put that more simply: …"* | 更简单地说…… | 发现自己绕进长句时**自救** |
| *"In other words, …"* | 换句话说…… | 换一种说法争取被理解 |
| *"What I mean is …"* | 我的意思是…… | 考官露出疑惑表情时立刻补 |
| *"Let me rephrase that — …"* | 我换个说法…… | 说完觉得不地道时**立即重说** |
| *"Put bluntly, …"* | 说白了…… | 需要一句概括收束时 |
| *"If I can put it in a picture: …"* | 打个比方…… | 抽象概念讲不清 → 上例子 |

### 5.3 Eingestehen & Korrigieren（承认空白并自救）

| 英语表达 | 中文 | 使用场景 |
|---|---|---|
| *"I am not certain about the exact figure, so I would rather not quote one."* | 具体数字我不确定，就不报了 | **不编数据**——诚实优于编造 |
| *"That is not something I have looked into in detail."* | 这个我没有深入查过 | 真不会时，立刻转到知道的 |
| *"I would have to check that — but what I can say is …"* | 这我得核实；但我能说的是…… | 承认 + **立刻给替代答案** |
| *"I overstated that slightly; let me correct myself."* | 我刚才说得有点过，我修正一下 | 自己发现说错时主动收回 |
| *"I do not have an example ready — may I come back to that?"* | 例子我一时没有，能稍后回来吗 | 争取在后面补上 |
| *"That lies a little outside what I prepared."* | 这稍微超出我的准备范围 | ⚠️ 说完**必须接** "but…" |

### 5.4 Überleiten & Strukturieren（拉回主线）

| 英语表达 | 中文 | 使用场景 |
|---|---|---|
| *"That connects to my second point, because …"* | 这接上了我的第二点，因为…… | 把追问**拉回自己的稿子** |
| *"This brings me back to my main claim: …"* | 这把我带回到我的核心主张…… | 跑题 30 秒后**强制收线** |
| *"Let me set that aside for a moment and finish the thought."* | 这点先放一放，我把话说完 | 考官打断时的礼貌收线 |
| *"So, to sum up where we are: …"* | 我们梳理一下现在到哪了…… | 中途小结，显出掌控力 |
| *"That is the point I wanted to make in part three."* | 这正是我第三部分要说的 | 自然接回原结构 |
| *"Let me close that thread before we move on."* | 我们先把这条线收掉 | 结束一个子话题 |

### 5.5 Zustimmen & Einräumen（先同意再反转，应对刁难）

| 英语表达 | 中文 | 使用场景 |
|---|---|---|
| *"I take your point — but I would put the emphasis elsewhere."* | 您说得对，但我想把重点放别处 | **① Concede & re-accentuate** |
| *"That is true up to a point; the difference I would insist on is …"* | 部分成立；我要坚持的区别是…… | **② Differentiate** |
| *"I would not go quite that far."* | 我不会说得那么绝对 | 温和地撤回过强表述 |
| *"You are right to press me on that — a better formulation would be …"* | 您追问得对，更准确的说法是…… | 被抓到漏洞时**主动升级说法** |
| *"I can see why that looks contradictory. Let me distinguish …"* | 看着确实矛盾，让我区分一下…… | **② Differentiate** 的正式版 |
| *"Fair enough — and one piece of evidence for my side would be …"* | 有道理；而我这边的一条证据是…… | **③ Supply evidence** |

### 5.6 Notfall-Satzbausteine（完全没思路时的 5 个万能句）

1. *"Lassen Sie mich das kurz einordnen — let me just place that in the wider context of my argument."*（把问题挂回自己的框架）
2. *"The honest answer is that I have not settled that question — but the distinction I would start from is …"*（承认未决 + 给出切入口）
3. *"Let me answer the question behind the question: I think what is really at stake is …"*（重述为更根本的问题）
4. *"I can give you a provisional answer, and I would want to revise it later: …"*（先给暂定答案，留修正余地）
5. *"Let me come at that from the other end — what would have to be true for my claim to fail?"*（反过来问，把压力变成分析）

### 5.7 禁忌清单（Tabu-Liste）

| 禁忌表达 | 为什么扣分 | 替代表达 |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ *"I have no idea."* | 直接放弃 → Gesprächsfähigkeit 最低档 | ✅ *"I have not looked into that specifically, but my starting assumption would be …"* |
| ❌ *"Das steht nicht im Material."* / *"It is not in the material."* | 显得只能复述材料 | ✅ *"The material does not say — so let me reason from the mechanism instead: …"* |
| ❌ *"That is what everyone says."* | 无来源的主张 | ✅ *"The standard argument is …; the reason I find it convincing is …"* |
| ❌ *"I think it is bad."*（无理由的判断） | AFB III 要求**有理据** | ✅ *"I would argue it is problematic because … — the mechanism being …"* |
| ❌ 编造数据／研究／人名 | 一旦被追问即全线崩塌，且违反学术诚信 | ✅ *"I do not have the exact figure; the direction, however, is clear from my material: …"* |

## 6. Selbstcheck & Glossar-Zeilen

### 6.1 录音自检清单（10 条）

1. **语速**：随机取 60 秒录音，数词数 → 应落在 **110–130 words/min**；超过 140 就是背书，低于 100 显得准备不足。
2. **时长**：Vortrag 完整跑一遍，控制在 **9:30–10:30**；提前结束比超时更伤。
3. **These 出现时间**：第 **60–90 秒**之内必须说出 `my claim is …` / `my thesis is …`。
4. **Redemittel 数量**：全篇至少 **12 个** discourse markers（`So, / That brings me to / What I would add, though, is / On balance`）。少于 8 个 → Darstellungsvermögen 降档。
5. **数字准确度**：3 条 Beleg（78→171 · 54 vs. 71 · 50 Minuten Schlaf）**一条都不错**。
6. **区分动作**：至少 **2 次**明确区分（`I would distinguish between … and …` / `not X as such, but …`）——这是本篇的得分核心。
7. **停顿质量**：`|` 处**真的停顿** 0.5 秒；连读不停顿 = 听不出结构。
8. **句长**：随机抽 5 句，每句 **≤ 25 words**；出现 35+ words 的长句立即拆成两句。
9. **Teil 2 四动作**：每次回答自查 Respond / Advance / Justify / Relaunch **四项是否齐全**；缺 Relaunch 补一句 `Would you say that also holds for …?`
10. **发音硬伤**：`algorithm`（**AL**-go-rithm，重音在第一音节）· `commodity` · `correlation/causation` · `specific` 四个词各读 3 遍。
