# Satzbausteine (Englisch Klausur — NRW Standard)

> **Purpose**: Essential phrases, connecting structures, and communicative templates for upper secondary English exams (Gymnasiale Oberstufe NRW, EF–Abitur).  
> **Evaluation Criteria**: Directly aligned with the official NRW criteria for **Content (Inhaltliche Leistung, 60%)** and **Language (Sprachliche Leistung / Darstellungsleistung, 40%)** (Focus: *Fluency, Register, Cohesion, Precision & Quoting*).

---

## 1. Task Orientation & Clear Text Structure (AFB I–III Architecture)

### 1.1 Introductory Sentence (The Classical TAWTE Scheme: Title, Author, Text Type, Topic, Date/Context)
- **Non-fictional Text (Article / Editorial / Speech)**:  
  “In the newspaper article *‘[Title]’*, published in *[Source]* on [Date], the author [Author's Name] addresses the contentious issue of [Topic] and argues that [Central Thesis].”
- **Political Speech**:  
  “In his keynote address delivered on [Occasion/Date] before [Audience], [Speaker's Name] appeals to [Target Group] to [Core Intention], asserting that [Main Claim].”
- **Fictional Text (Novel Excerpt / Short Story / Drama)**:  
  “The excerpt taken from [Author]’s novel/drama *‘[Title]’* ([Year]) illustrates the emotional and ideological conflict between [Character A] and [Character B] regarding [Conflict Source].”

### 1.2 Structuring the Three Sub-Tasks (AFB I–III)
- **Part 1 (Comprehension / Summary — AFB I)**:  
  - “To begin with, the article will be summarized by outlining the author's primary line of reasoning.”
  - “The following analysis is prefaced by a structured account of the protagonist’s evolving attitude towards [Problem].”
- **Part 2 (Analysis of Rhetorical Devices & Stylistic Strategies — AFB II)**:  
  - “In the subsequent step, the text will be examined regarding its argumentative strategies, stylistic choices, and intended emotional impact on the reader.”
  - “Focusing on the use of imagery and syntactical structure, this section analyzes how the speaker conveys his vision of [Concept].”
- **Part 3 (Evaluation: Comment / Discussion / Re-creation of Text — AFB III)**:  
  - “Building on the analysis, the author’s proposition that [Claim] will be critically assessed in a structured comment.”
  - “Finally, the essay discusses the transferability of this model to contemporary British/American society by weighing conflicting perspectives.”

## 2. Separation of Layers: Description, Analysis, and Personal Judgement

### 2.1 Descriptive Layer (Summary & Context — Objective, Neutral, Simple Present)
- “The text can be subdivided into three main thematic sections: Initially (ll. 1–14), the narrator describes [...].”
- “The author sets out by pointing out that global migration patterns have shifted considerably over the past decade.”
- “According to the speaker, the root cause of this dilemma lies in institutional negligence (cf. l. 25).”

### 2.2 Analytical Layer (Function, Stylistic Impact & Reader Guidance)
- “By employing a cumulative enumeration of grievances (ll. 12–15), the speaker accentuates the gravity of the socioeconomic crisis.”
- “On a structural level, the antithesis between ‘[Word A]’ and ‘[Word B]’ (l. 34) polarizes the audience, effectively creating an ‘us-versus-them’ dichotomy.”
- “The metaphorical portrayal of technological advancement as an ‘unstoppable avalanche’ (l. 42) evokes a sense of urgency and apprehension among the readership.”
- “The deliberate choice of informal colloquialisms serves to bridge the gap between the intellectual elite and working-class citizens.”

### 2.3 Evaluative Layer (Critical Reflection & Balanced Evaluation)
- “From a utilitarian viewpoint, this measure appears justifiable; however, it severely encroaches upon individual privacy rights.”
- “Weighing the undeniable economic benefits against the ethical hazards, the argument regarding human dignity carries greater moral weight.”
- “While the author's diagnosis of social polarization is compelling, his proposed remedy remains fundamentally utopian and detached from political realities.”

## 3. Academic Quoting & In-Text Documentation Technique

### 3.1 Smooth Syntactic Integration of Direct Quotes (No Quote Islands!)
- **Seamlessly Embedded Quote**:  
  The protagonist perceives the urban environment as “a suffocating labyrinth of concrete and greed” (l. 18), which underscores his profound psychological isolation.
- **Adjusting Grammar with Brackets**:  
  The speaker contends that “[these] systematic inequalities demand immediate legislative action” (ll. 45f.).
- **Paraphrasing with Indirect Citation**:  
  Moreover, the study indicates that digital hyperconnectivity tends to impair adolescent mental resilience (cf. ll. 67–71).

### 3.2 Reporting Verbs (Avoid Monotonous ‘The author says/writes’)
| Monotonous & Weak | Academically Precise Reporting Verbs |
|---|---|
| The author says that... | The author **argues / contends / maintains / posits / asserts** that... |
| He shows that... | He **elucidates / demonstrates / illustrates / exemplifies** that... |
| He criticizes... | He **denounces / calls into question / deplores / casts doubt on**... |
| He wants the reader to... | The writer **prompts / impels / urges the reader to** reflect on... |

## 4. Subject-Specific Vocabulary & Conceptual Frameworks

### 4.1 Global Challenges & Diversity (Core Themes in NRW EF/Q-Phase)
- **Cultural Identity & Postcolonialism**: acculturation, assimilation, cultural hybridity, marginalization, imperial legacy, multicultural cohesion vs. parallel societies.
- **Globalization & Economic Disparities**: multinational corporations, outsourced labor, ecological footprint, fair-trade equilibrium, structural dependency.
- **Media & The Digital Age**: digital surveillance, filter bubbles, algorithmic radicalization, disinformation, cognitive overload.
- **Visions of the Future / Dystopia**: totalitarian surveillance, dehumanization, technological deterministic hubris, environmental degradation.

### 4.2 Rhetorical Devices & Communicative Effects
- **Alliteration**: lends a rhythmic, memorable quality to key slogans.
- **Anaphora**: emphasizes collective determination and builds rhythmic momentum.
- **Hypophora**: allows the speaker to preempt potential counterarguments and establish intellectual authority.
- **Inclusive Pronouns (‘we’, ‘our’)**: foster a sense of solidarity and collective responsibility.

## 5. Cohesion, Sentence Variety & Academic Elegance

### 5.1 Concessive & Contrastive Connectors
- “Albeit valid at first glance, this objection fails to account for long-term sociological repercussions.”
- “Notwithstanding the considerable fiscal costs involved, the moral imperative remains indisputable.”
- “In stark contrast to traditional paradigms, the novel portrays vulnerability not as a deficiency, but as a catalyst for growth.”

### 5.2 Causal & Consecutive Linkers
- “Consequently, systemic barriers perpetuate generational poverty, culminating in widespread disillusionment.”
- “This phenomenon can be largely attributed to the deregulation of financial markets in the late 1990s.”
- “It follows logically from these premises that cosmetic reforms will prove insufficient.”

### 5.3 Concluding Formulae for the Final Judgement
- “Taking all dimensions into consideration, one arrives at the conclusion that while technological autonomy offers unprecedented opportunities, it necessitates robust democratic oversight.”
- “In the final analysis, the author's appeal serves less as a pragmatic policy roadmap than as a moral wake-up call to civil society.”
