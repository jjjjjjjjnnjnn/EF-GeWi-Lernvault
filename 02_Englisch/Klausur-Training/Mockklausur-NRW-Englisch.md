## 2. Material

**The Classroom Algorithm: Why Schools Must Teach _With_ Machines, Not Against Them**

> ⚠️ 原创仿写 Modelltext（仿 *British broadsheet* opinion-editorial / Kommentarspalte 风格撰写，非真实出版物摘录；数据与人名均为虚构）
> ⚠️ fiktiv：Autorin „Dr. Eleanor Whitcombe"、"Centre for Digital Pedagogy"、Ruhr-Gesamtschule、Umfrage 2025 — **均为虚构**，仅用于材料仿真，不得作为真实来源引用。

*by Dr. Eleanor Whitcombe, Centre for Digital Pedagogy (fictitious)*

**[1]** When a fifteen-year-old in Dortmund asks a chatbot to explain the subjunctive, she is not cheating. She is doing what pupils have always done: reaching for the nearest tool that will get the job done. The difference is that this one talks back. Across North Rhine-Westphalia, teachers estimate that roughly two thirds of their students have used generative artificial intelligence for homework at least once; in a survey of 1,240 secondary teachers conducted in 2025, 68 per cent reported having found machine-generated passages in submitted coursework. Panic is the wrong response. Banning the software is not merely futile; it is pedagogically irresponsible. My claim is straightforward: schools must teach *with* these machines and, at the same time, teach their students to think *against* them.

**[2]** The first argument is the least romantic one: prohibition has never worked. Every school I visit has a telephone policy, and every school I visit has telephones. The same logic governs software that lives in a browser tab. A ban does not remove the technology from a young person's life; it removes the teacher from the conversation about how the technology is used. Where schools have answered with detection software instead, the outcome has been worse still: false accusations, adversarial classrooms, and an arms race in which the detectors are permanently one generation behind.

**[3]** The second argument is stronger. For half a century, comprehensive schools have promised individualised support and delivered it mainly to those whose parents could buy it privately. An AI tutor costs nothing at the margin, never tires, and will explain the same quadratic equation eleven times without sighing. In a twelve-week trial at a comprehensive school in the Ruhr area, pupils working with a supervised writing assistant improved the structural organisation of their essays by 31 per cent; a comparison group improved by 9 per cent. Individual attention, once a luxury, becomes a default.

**[4]** Beneath this lies a harder point. If a machine can produce a competent essay on *Macbeth*, then the traditional take-home essay was never measuring thought in the first place. It was measuring stamina, compliance, and access to a quiet room. That verdict is uncomfortable, but it is also a gift: it forces us to ask what only a human being can do in a classroom — frame a question, defend a judgement, and change one's mind in public.

**[5]** Equity cuts both ways, and honesty demands that we say so. Artificial intelligence narrows the tutoring gap for families who cannot afford a tutor, and it widens the surveillance gap for everyone: every prompt a child types is data, logged, mined and sold. Unless access is publicly funded and commercially clean, we will have handed the least powerful pupils a free tutor and a permanent dossier.

**[6]** That brings me to the price. Teaching cannot be reduced to moderating a machine. A teacher who reads an essay knowing that a pupil wrote it imperfectly, in their own voice, learns something about that pupil; a teacher who reads machine-perfect prose learns nothing at all. The relationship is the curriculum.

**[7]** The task, then, is neither adoption nor refusal. It is critical AI literacy: pupils who can interrogate a source, name its bias, and state plainly what they wrote and what they did not. The generation now sitting in our classrooms will work alongside systems we cannot yet imagine. They may forgive us our caution. They will not forgive us for sending them into that future unarmed.

`[Wortzahl: 573]`

**Aufgabe 1 [AFB I] · Operator: outline (darstellen) · 12 BE · 15 min**

> **DE:** Stellen Sie die zentrale These der Autorin sowie die vier Argumente, mit denen sie diese These stützt, in eigenen Worten dar.
> **EN (Originalwortlaut):** *Outline the author's central claim and the four arguments she puts forward to support it.*

中文提示：考"能否把四条论据从 600 词里**抽出来并压缩转述**"（AFB I 的 outline = 只转述、不评论）。陷阱：① 把 68 %/31 % 之类数据当"论据"抄一通；② 把自己的看法混进去（outline 里出现 *I think* 直接扣分）；③ 时态用错（转述一律 **present tense**）。

---

**Aufgabe 2 [AFB I] · Operator: sum up (wiedergeben) · 10 BE · 10 min**

> **DE:** Geben Sie die Ausgangslage wieder, die die Autorin im ersten Abschnitt zeichnet, und benennen Sie die zentralen Daten.
> **EN:** *Sum up the situation in schools as outlined in the first paragraph and state the key figures.*

中文提示：考信息定位 + 复述（own words, present tense）。陷阱：整句照抄原句（算 *copying*，不得分）；把虚构调查当真实来源引用（应写 *the (fictional) survey*）；漏掉"学校的两种反应：禁令 + 检测软件"。

---

**Aufgabe 3 [AFB I] · Operator: identify (benennen) · 8 BE · 10 min**

> **DE:** Benennen Sie Textsorte und Adressatenschaft des Textes sowie vier rhetorische Mittel mit Textbeleg.
> **EN:** *Identify the text type and the intended audience, and name four rhetorical devices, giving a brief quotation for each.*

中文提示：这一层**只要"名称 + 位置 + 短引"**，不要求写效果（效果留给 Aufgabe 4）。陷阱：只写 metaphor / irony 两个"万能词"凑数；写了名称却没给 Beleg；把 Textsorte 写成 *report* 或 *scientific article*。

---

**Aufgabe 4 [AFB II] · Operator: analyse (analysieren) · 20 BE · 25 min**

> **DE:** Analysieren Sie, wie die Autorin mit sprachlich-rhetorischen Mitteln ihre Botschaft vermittelt. Beziehen Sie sich auf die Eingangsanekdote, die Antithese, die Metaphorik und den Schlussappell.
> **EN:** *Analyse how the author uses language and rhetorical devices to convey her message. Focus on the opening anecdote, the antithesis, her use of metaphor, and the closing appeal.*

中文提示：考 **P.E.E. 三步**（Point → Evidence → Effect）写满四点。陷阱：只列手段不写效果；效果写成空话（*"it makes the text interesting"*）；Effect 必须**回扣 These**（这些手段如何让读者接受"既要用、又要批判"这个立场）。

---

**Aufgabe 5 [AFB II] · Operator: examine (untersuchen) · 14 BE · 20 min**

> **DE:** Untersuchen Sie den Gedankengang des Textes, insbesondere die Einbindung des Gegenarguments im fünften Abschnitt.
> **EN:** *Examine the author's line of argument, paying particular attention to how she integrates the counterargument in paragraph 5.*

中文提示：考论证**结构**（these-first → 由实用到规范的递进 → 让步 → 收束）与**策略**。陷阱：把 [5] 的 Konzession（作者主动让步）误读成"作者自己的反对意见"；只复述内容、不写"这样安排有什么用"。

---

**Aufgabe 6 [AFB II] · Operator: compare (vergleichen) · 16 BE · 20 min**

> **DE:** Vergleichen Sie die Position der Autorin mit der untenstehenden Gegenposition und arbeiten Sie heraus, worin sich beide in ihren Prämissen unterscheiden.
> **EN:** *Compare the author's position with the opposing view below and work out where the two differ in terms of their underlying premises.*

> **Gegenposition (⚠️ fiktiv)** — Auszug aus einem fiktiven Elternbrief einer Gesamtschule in Münster:
> *"A machine that writes for our children teaches them nothing. School is the one place where a young person must struggle alone with a difficult sentence, because the struggle **is** the lesson. If we let software do the thinking, we produce pupils who can generate text but cannot defend a single idea of their own. AI has no place in the classroom."*

中文提示：考"在**前提层面**做比较"——两边都认同"学校要培养独立思考"，分歧只在**路径**（在她：反思机器输出；在家长：脱离机器亲手写）。陷阱：只把两边观点各复述一遍（那叫 summary，不是 compare）；不做抽象、不点出 gemeinsame Prämisse。

---

**Aufgabe 7 [AFB III] · Operator: evaluate / take a stance (Stellung nehmen, erörtern) · 20 BE · 30 min**

> **DE:** Nehmen Sie begründet Stellung zu der Frage, ob generative KI in den Unterricht integriert werden sollte. Erörtern Sie die Position der Autorin kritisch und begründen Sie Ihr eigenes Urteil.
> **EN:** *Take a stance: Evaluate whether generative AI should be integrated into classroom practice. Discuss the author's claim critically and give reasons for your own judgement.*

中文提示：考 AFB III 完整五步：**These → Begründung(+Beispiel) → Gegenargument → Abwägung → begründetes Urteil**。陷阱：① 骑墙结论（*"both sides have a point"* 直接掉到 AFB II）；② 把作者观点当成自己的观点复述一遍；③ 反方写成稻草人（*"some people are simply afraid of technology"*）；④ 结论不给条件/标准。

## 6. Musterlösung (标准答案要点 · English)

> **Sprachliche Grundregeln (gilt für alle Aufgaben)**：Summary/Analysis im **present tense**；Zitate kursiv + in Anführungszeichen；Belege als „[Abschnitt] *Kurzzitat*"；keine Ich-Bewertung in AFB I/II；Register: akademisch, contractions vermeiden (*do not*, nicht *don't*)。

### 6.1 Set A — Analysis (Aufgaben 1–6; Schwerpunkt A4–A6)

**A1 — Outline (AFB I, 12 BE)**

- **Claim**: *„schools must teach with these machines and … teach their students to think against them"* [1] — neither prohibition nor uncritical adoption.
- **Argument 1 (pragmatic)**: bans have never worked; detection software produces false accusations and an „arms race" [2].
- **Argument 2 (pedagogical)**: AI tutors deliver the individualised support comprehensive schools have long promised — the Ruhr trial reports 31 % vs. 9 % improvement [3].
- **Argument 3 (assessment)**: if a machine can write a competent *Macbeth* essay, the take-home essay measured stamina, not thought [4].
- **Argument 4 (equity)**: AI narrows the tutoring gap but widens the surveillance gap [5].
- **Klausur-Satz**: *The author argues that schools ought neither to ban nor to embrace generative AI uncritically; instead, they should work with it while training pupils to scrutinise its output.*

**A2 — Sum up (AFB I, 10 BE)**

- Pupils in NRW already use generative AI for homework; about **two thirds** have done so at least once, and **68 % of 1,240** teachers surveyed in 2025 found machine-generated passages in coursework [1].
- Schools have reacted with **bans** and, increasingly, with **detection software**.
- The author rejects this reaction: panic is misplaced and banning is „pedagogically irresponsible".

**A3 — Identify (AFB I, 8 BE)**

- **Text type**: opinion-editorial / comment piece in a nationally circulating newspaper; appellative, thesis-driven.
- **Audience**: teachers, school leaders, parents, education policymakers.
- **Devices**: individualising anecdote [1] · antithesis [1]/[7] · metaphor „arms race" [2] · tricolon „stamina, compliance, and access to a quiet room" [4] · concession [5] · archaic closing appeal [7].

**A4 — Analyse (AFB II, 20 BE) — P.E.E.-Muster**

1. *The author opens with an individualising anecdote: a fifteen-year-old in Dortmund who asks a chatbot to explain the subjunctive* [1]. *By presenting the issue through one identifiable pupil rather than through an abstraction, she makes the phenomenon tangible and, crucially, reframes it: the girl „is not cheating", she is reaching for the nearest tool. The effect is that readers are denied the moral reflex of condemnation before the argument has even begun.*
2. *Her thesis is cast as an antithesis — „teach with these machines and … think against them"* [1] — *which is echoed in the closing „neither adoption nor refusal"* [7]. *The parallel construction compresses a complex position into a memorable formula and signals balance, so that the reader registers the claim as moderate rather than techno-enthusiastic.*
3. *The metaphor of the „arms race", in which detectors are „permanently one generation behind"* [2], *recasts the debate as a futile contest of escalation. Because the image returns in the closing line — pupils must not be sent into the future „unarmed"* [7] — *the text is framed by a single conceptual field; this ring composition turns a technical problem into a moral obligation to equip rather than to protect.*
4. *The closing appeal resorts to an anaphoric, almost biblical pairing: „They may forgive us our caution. They will not forgive us …"* [7]. *The elevated register and the shift to the collective „us" create moral urgency and transfer responsibility from the technology to the adults who decide about it.*

**A5 — Examine (AFB II, 14 BE)**

- **Structure**: thesis at the end of [1] (*these-first*), then four supporting arguments in ascending order.
- **Escalation**: pragmatic ([2] prohibition fails) → pedagogical ([3][4] support and assessment) → normative ([5][6] equity and the teacher–pupil relationship).
- **Concession**: [5] „Equity cuts both ways" — the author raises the surveillance objection herself and answers it with a condition (publicly funded, „commercially clean" access).
- **Effect**: the concession establishes ethos; it defuses the charge of techno-optimism and attaches her own claim to conditions, which makes the closing demand for „critical AI literacy" appear as the logical consequence rather than as a slogan.

**A6 — Compare (AFB II, 16 BE)**

- **Opposing view**: school is the place of solitary struggle; the struggle *is* the lesson; machine-generated text produces pupils who cannot defend an idea.
- **Shared premise**: both sides regard independent thinking as the core purpose of schooling — they disagree about the **route**, not the **destination**.
- **Divergence**: the author locates thinking in the **critical interrogation of machine output**; the parents' letter locates it in **unaided production**.
- **Consequence**: because the dispute is about means, the author's position stands or falls with its conditions — public funding and data protection. Where those conditions are absent, the parents' objection regains its force.

### 6.2 Set B — Evaluation / Comment (Aufgabe 7, 20 BE)

> **Pflichtbausteine (五步)**：These → Begründung + Beispiel → Gegenargument → Abwägung → begründetes Urteil (mit Bedingung)。下面给**两种可写立场**的骨架，任选其一，**不要**混写。

**Variante A — *Conditional integration* (贴近作者立场，但加条件)**

- **These**: *Generative AI should be integrated into classroom practice, but only where its use is transparent, supervised and publicly funded.*
- **Begründung 1 (Lernwirksamkeit)**: *Used as a tutor rather than as a ghostwriter, AI gives every pupil the individual feedback that comprehensive schools have promised for decades. In my own experience, explaining a grammar rule to a chatbot and then correcting its answer forces me to formulate the rule myself.*
- **Begründung 2 (Gerechtigkeit)**: *Private tutoring is the single most unequal resource in German schooling; a free, supervised tool narrows that gap — provided access does not depend on the quality of a family's device or subscription.*
- **Gegenargument**: *Critics are right that outsourcing the struggle removes the struggle itself; a pupil who has never wrestled with a sentence has never owned an idea.*
- **Abwägung**: *The decisive criterion is therefore not whether AI is used, but **what is assessed**: if marks reward the product, the tool replaces the learner; if marks reward the process — drafting, revising, defending — the tool becomes a training partner.*
- **Urteil**: *I therefore endorse the author's claim with one amendment: integration without a change in assessment culture merely automates cheating. AI belongs in the classroom only where schools are willing to assess thinking rather than text.*

**Variante B — *Restricted use* (批判作者立场)**

- **These**: *Whitcombe's conclusion is persuasive in theory but premature in practice: generative AI should be admitted to classrooms only for narrowly defined feedback tasks, not as a general learning companion.*
- **Begründung 1 (Datenethik)**: *Every prompt is data; until providers are contractually barred from training on pupils' input, „free" tutoring is paid for with surveillance — and minors cannot consent to that.*
- **Begründung 2 (Kompetenzaufbau)**: *Competence is built by retrieval and by failure. A tool that „will explain the same quadratic equation eleven times without sighing"* [3] *removes precisely the friction that consolidates understanding.*
- **Gegenargument (fair)**: *This is not an argument for prohibition. The author is right that bans drive the practice underground and that detection tools punish the innocent; the choice is not between prohibition and enthusiasm.*
- **Abwägung**: *What matters is the sequence: first secure the basics — handwriting a paragraph, solving an equation unaided — and only then introduce the machine as a critic of one's own work.*
- **Urteil**: *On balance, Whitcombe underestimates her own fifth paragraph: the surveillance gap she names is not a side effect but a structural risk. Integration is defensible; the timeline she implies is not.*

> **Klausur-Satz-Baukasten (Deutsch → English)**:
> *„Ich vertrete die Auffassung, dass …"* → **I take the view that …** · *„Ein gewichtiges Gegenargument lautet …"* → **A weighty objection is that …** · *„Abwägend ist entscheidend, dass …"* → **What tips the balance is that …** · *„Daraus folgt, dass … nur unter der Bedingung …"* → **It follows that … only on condition that …**

### 7.2 「保 80 分」三条（AFB I+II 的可复制套路）

1. **A1 用"1 句 These + 4 句 Argument"五句式**：每句一个论据，句首用 *First/Second/Third/Finally*，句尾挂段号。不写细节数据 → 细节留给 A2。这样 12 BE 拿 10+。
2. **A4 严格 P.E.E.，四点各写 4–5 行**：Point（手段名称）→ Evidence（[段号] + 短引）→ **Effect 必须回扣 These**（*this presents her position as moderate / this turns a technical issue into a moral obligation*）。缺 Effect = 每点只给一半分。
3. **A5/A6 用"结构词 + 前提词"抓分**：A5 写 *thesis-first → escalation (pragmatic → pedagogical → normative) → concession*；A6 写 *shared premise → divergence → consequence*。这两题的分数几乎全在术语上，术语对了内容平庸也能拿 70 %。

### 7.3 「抢 20 分」三条（AFB III 五步法）

1. **第一步就亮立场**：第一句必须是 *I take the view that …* / *On balance, I would argue that …*，禁止 *There are two sides* 开头。
2. **反方要"强"**：把 Gegenargument 写成最聪明版本（*critics are right that outsourcing the struggle removes the struggle itself*），再用 **Kriterium**（Lernwirksamkeit / Gerechtigkeit / Datenethik / Prüfbarkeit）压过去——这一步是 13–15 分与 8–10 分的分水岭。
3. **结论带条件**：*only on condition that …* / *provided that …*。有条件的有条件句 = 论证深度的直接证据，且天然避免"骑墙"扣分。

### 7.4 高频失分点三条

1. **Summary 里混入评论**（A1/A2 出现 *I think / in my opinion*）→ AFB I 直接掉档；评论只能出现在 A7。
2. **只列手段不写效果，或效果写成空话**（*"makes the text interesting / nicer"*）→ A4 每点扣 2–3 BE。
3. **A7 骑墙或把作者观点当自己的写**：判卷第一眼看的是"有没有独立立场"，没有立场最高只能进 AFB II 档（≈ 10/20 BE）。
