# Lernreise: Comment Writing: P.E.E. und Register (L1, Ziel Klausur)

## Schritt 1 — entdecken

OBJECTIVES (three measurable goals of this lesson):

1. You can explain the four-part **comment skeleton** (Stellungnahme) — introduction, position, arguments, conclusion — and why a comment must judge instead of summarising.
2. You can run **P.E.E.** (Point, Evidence, Explain) for every argument, especially the often-missing Explain step.
3. You can write in **formal and semi-formal register** (Register) within 150-200 words, checked with linking words.

### Hook / Phenomenon

Six opinions in 180 words feel productive and score like a list. Three explained reasons feel slower and earn the judgement marks. Why does depth beat number under a word limit?

### Core Concept & Definition

A **comment** (Stellungnahme) is **evaluated writing of 150-200 words** that earns its judgement through **position, reasons and fairness**. It never reproduces the text but argues a verdict with **P.E.E. chains and one counterargument**. The skeleton is fixed: topic plus statement, clear thesis, two or three proven points, summary plus final judgement. Listing without explaining stays on AFB I.

### Causal Chain / Model

The chain runs: **position, proof, fairness, close**. First, the position is fixed in one sentence at the end of the introduction. Second, each argument runs full P.E.E. with concrete example and an Explain sentence linking reason to position. Third, one opposing view is treated fairly and answered to secure fairness marks. Fourth, the conclusion summarises and judges with balance. Two decisions precede drafting: my position and my reader.

Klausur-Satz: `A comment does not list opinions; it earns its judgement through a clear position, explained reasons, concrete examples and one fair counterargument.`

## Schritt 2 — entdecken

PRETRAINING-Box (five core terms with definitions):

### Hook / Phenomenon

"Firstly, social media is harmful" — then silence. The point stands alone, the example missing, the link unspoken. Most comments do not lack views, they lack the sentence that turns a view into a reason.

### Core Terms & Definitions

- **Comment** (Stellungnahme): **150-200 words of evaluated writing**; it must judge, not reproduce the text.
- **Position** (thesis statement): **one clear sentence** at the end of the introduction stating agreement or disagreement.
- **P.E.E.** (Point, Evidence, Explain): **three-step chain per argument**; without Explain the point stays on AFB I.
- **Counterargument** (Gegenargument): at least **one opposing view treated fairly**; required for AFB III fairness marks.
- **Register:** **formal or semi-formal tone** decided by reader (Adressat) and occasion; formal bans contractions and slang.

### How They Connect / Model

The terms form one verdict machine: the **position** aims it, **P.E.E.** loads each barrel with example and link, the **counterargument** proves fairness, the **register** fits the shot to the reader. A **comment** without Explain misfires; without counterargument it shoots one-sidedly; with drifted register it shoots in the wrong uniform. Depth plus fairness plus fit score.

Klausur-Satz: `Before writing, I decide on my position and the register, because a comment must be clearly evaluative and appropriate for its audience.`

## Schritt 3 — entdecken

TIEFEN-KONZEPT (one concept with visual schema):

### Hook / Phenomenon

English has no academy fixing correctness, yet every teacher marks "gonna" in a formal letter. If no institution decides, who does? Reader and occasion — exactly what writers must check first.

### Core Concept & Definition

**Register fit as appropriateness gate** means that **reader and occasion decide formal versus semi-formal** before the first sentence. Its formula runs $Fit = Reader + Occasion$. A formal letter to adults bans contractions and slang; a youth-forum post allows direct address and contractions. Content can be perfect and still fail when the uniform is wrong.

### Causal Chain / Model

The chain runs: **reader, choice, consistency, judgement**. First, the addressee is fixed as official adult or peer group. Second, the register is chosen and contractions are allowed or banned. Third, the choice is kept consistent from salutation to closing without drift into slang. Fourth, linking words secure the flow while P.E.E. secures the proof. Fit frames proof; drift breaks it.

```diagram
  COMMENT (150-200 words) — four parts plus gate
  1. Introduction: topic + restated statement
  2. Position: "In my opinion, ..." (clear thesis)
  3. Arguments: 2-3 points, each as P.E.E.
     Point -> Evidence (example) -> Explain (WHY proof)
     + counterargument: "However, critics argue ..."
  4. Conclusion: summary + "On balance, I conclude ..."
  REGISTER gate: formal (no contractions) vs. semi-formal
```

   COMMENT (Stellungnahme, 150-200 words)

Klausur-Satz: `A convincing comment follows the four-part structure and supports every argument with the P.E.E. chain, including the counterargument that shows fairness.`

## Anekdote & Fun-Fact

**Anekdote / Fun-Fact (EN)**: English has no official language academy. Unlike French with its Academie francaise or German with its spelling council, English is regulated by no state institution. What counts as correct or appropriate is decided by usage and situation alone.

**Bezug zum Konzept**: Since English has no academy to fix its register, appropriateness is decided by reader and occasion — exactly the question a comment writer must answer before drafting.

## Schritt 4 — ausprobieren

MODEL TASK (worked example with solution):

[Werkzeug: balance]

TASK (comment, AFB III): Write a comment (150-200 words) on the statement: "The best role models are people who show both their strengths and their weaknesses." Use the four-part structure, P.E.E. and one counterargument.

HELP:
1. Phrase your position in one sentence first (for example "I largely agree, because ...").
2. Collect two arguments for and one against; note one concrete example per point.
3. Write each point as P.E.E.; check finally whether every Explain sentence states why the point supports your position.

MODEL SOLUTION: Introduction: The statement claims that the best role models are those who show both their strengths and their weaknesses. Position: In my opinion, this is largely true, because imperfect role models are easier to relate to. Argument 1 (P.E.E.): Firstly, showing weaknesses makes a role model authentic. Evidence: When a famous athlete openly talks about a defeat, young fans see that failure is normal. Explain: This authenticity matters because teenagers compare themselves to idols constantly; a flawless idol only increases pressure, whereas a flawed one offers a realistic model. Argument 2 (P.E.E.): Secondly, admitting weaknesses teaches a valuable lesson. Evidence: A teacher who says "I do not know that yet, let us find out together" models lifelong learning. Explain: This supports my position because it turns the role model from a finished product into a process young people can imitate. Counterargument: However, critics argue that role models should set high standards, and that public weaknesses can excuse bad behaviour. Refutation: Yet a weakness honestly admitted is not the same as misconduct excused; the difference lies in whether the person takes responsibility. Conclusion: On balance, I therefore conclude that the best role models are credible rather than perfect, since credibility, not flawlessness, is what young people can actually learn from.

Klausur-Satz: `On balance, I would argue that credible role models who admit their weaknesses are more useful to young people than flawless idols, because authenticity can be imitated while perfection cannot.`

## Schritt 5 — ausprobieren

COMPARISON (two procedures):

COMPARISON: Decide first — (i) outline procedure (neutral reproduction, AFB I) or (ii) comment procedure (own judgement with reasons, AFB III) — then write.

TASK A: Outline the author's main arguments about role models in the text above.
TASK B: Comment on whether social media makes young people more or less confident.

HELP: A requires outline — neutral reproduction, no judgement, present tense, own words -> procedure (i). B requires comment — own position, P.E.E., counterargument, final judgement -> procedure (ii). Rule: outline and sum up mean reproduce; comment, discuss, assess and evaluate mean judge.

ANSWER: A requires procedure (i): I reproduce the author arguments in my own words and in the present tense, without "I think" or "in my opinion". A verdict here would be a structural error, because outline only reproduces. B requires procedure (ii): I state a clear position ("In my opinion, social media makes teenagers less confident, although it also offers support"), support it with two P.E.E. arguments (comparison pressure through perfect images — evidence from screen-time studies — explain: constant comparison lowers self-esteem), concede a counterargument (online community and role models) and close with a final judgement. The difference lies not in content but in task: outline reproduces what the text says, comment judges what I think.

Klausur-Satz: `The same topic can be handled in two ways: outline reproduces the author's points neutrally, whereas comment requires my own reasoned judgement with a counterargument.`

## Schritt 6 — check

CHECK (three questions with answers):

FRAGE: Which four parts form a comment? | ANTWORT: Introduction (topic plus statement), position (clear thesis), arguments (two to three P.E.E. points plus counterargument) and conclusion (summary plus final judgement).
FRAGE: What is missing when an argument holds only Point and Evidence? | ANTWORT: The Explain step, the reason why the point supports the position; without it the argument stays on AFB I.
FRAGE: What decides the register? | ANTWORT: Reader and occasion: a formal letter requires formal register without contractions; a youth-forum post may use semi-formal style with direct address.

Klausur-Satz: `A comment loses marks when arguments lack the Explain step or when the register does not match the addressee.`

## Fehlvorstellung

1. Error: "A comment lists as many views as possible."
   Correction: Examiners mark depth, not number. Three fully explained points beat six listed ones; the 150-200 word limit is part of the task and overlength signals lost control.
   Korrektur-Satz: `Three arguments that are fully explained earn more marks than six points that are merely listed, and the word limit is part of the task.`

2. Error: "One side is enough; the counterargument is optional."
   Correction: AFB III requires fairness. A comment without a counterargument reads as one-sided pleading and loses judgement marks. The opposing view must be treated seriously and answered.
   Korrektur-Satz: `A fair comment must seriously consider one counterargument, even if the writer finally rejects it.`

## Schritt 8 — entdecken

TAKEAWAY (core summary):

A comment argues a verdict; it never reproduces or lists. The skeleton — introduction, position, arguments, conclusion — is obligatory; every point runs full P.E.E., especially the Explain step that links reason to position; one counterargument secures fairness. Fix position and reader before drafting: a comment scores on explained reasons, not on novel views.
Takeaway-Satz: `A comment earns its judgement: clear position, fully explained arguments, one fair counterargument and a register that fits the reader.`
