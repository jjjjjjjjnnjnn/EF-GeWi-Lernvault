### Hook / Phaenomen

Midnight on the battlements of Kronborg Castle in Elsinore. A bitter, biting wind sweeps across the dark waters of the Baltic Sea. Prince Hamlet of Denmark has just encountered the restless ghost of his murdered father, who demands bloody, immediate revenge against the fratricidal usurper Claudius. In any conventional Elizabethan revenge tragedy, the hero would instantly draw his rapier, charge into the throne room, and paint the castle walls with the villain's blood. But Hamlet does nothing of the sort. He is not a blunt medieval warrior; he is a student of philosophy from the University of Wittenberg – a Renaissance thinker whose razor-sharp intellect questions every assumption, doubts the spectral apparition, feigns madness, and dissects every possible consequence of his actions. Instead of a swift execution, the audience witnesses a agonizing psychological abyss: the most brilliant intellect in world literature is completely paralyzed by his own hyper-conscious introspection.

`Klausur-Satz: Hamlet personifies the modern existential anti-hero whose acute self-reflection and philosophical scruples paralyze his capacity for decisive moral action.`

## Schritt 2 — entdecken: The Shakespearean Dramatic Toolbox
PRETRAINING术语盒（核心4词，先读三遍中英，合书自测中文→英语）：

- 悲剧性缺陷 — Tragic Flaw (Hamartia): 亚里士多德与文艺复兴悲剧理论中导致高尚主角不可避免走向毁灭的核心性格缺陷或判断失误（哈姆雷特的缺陷即为过度思虑而行动瘫痪）。 The fatal character flaw or moral weakness that leads inevitably to the downfall of an otherwise noble tragic protagonist.
- 独白 — Soliloquy: 剧中角色在舞台上独处时直接向观众大声倾诉内心深层矛盾、真实动机与哲学思考的戏剧表达手法。 A dramatic device whereby a character speaks their innermost thoughts and anxieties aloud while alone on stage, revealing psychological authenticity.
- 素体五音步 / 无韵抑扬格 — Blank Verse: 莎士比亚戏剧中最为核心的诗体形式，由不押韵的十音节抑扬格构成，既贴近自然口语律动又兼具庄严崇高感。 Unrhymed iambic pentameter consisting of five metrical feet of unstressed followed by stressed syllables (da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM).
- 戏剧反讽 — Dramatic Irony: 舞台上的观众掌握了关键真相（如克劳狄斯确实毒杀了先王），而剧中其他人物却蒙在鼓里所形成的强烈张力状态。 A theatrical situation where the audience possesses critical knowledge of which the characters on stage remain unaware.

## Schritt 4 — ausprobieren: Close Reading Workshop on Hamlet's Speech

[Werkzeug: highlighter]

AUFGABE (analyze & interpret, AFB I/II):
Examine the following lines from Act III, Scene 1:
"Whether 'tis nobler in the mind to suffer / The slings and arrows of outrageous fortune, / Or to take arms against a sea of troubles, / And by opposing end them."
1. Identify the central metaphors used to represent human existence and suffering.
2. Analyze the syntactical paradox in the phrase "to take arms against a sea of troubles".

MUSTERLOESUNG:
1. Metaphorical Analysis:
   - "Slings and arrows of outrageous fortune": Fortune (fate) is personified as a hostile, siege-laying army raining down random, unprovoked missile strikes upon a passive victim.
   - Life is depicted not as an opportunity for achievement, but as an endurance test of defensive suffering.
2. Syntactical Paradox / Mixed Metaphor:
   - "To take arms against a sea of troubles": You can take up arms (weapons) against soldiers, but taking up a sword or shield against an infinite, engulfing body of water (the sea) is logically impossible and suicidal.
   - Through this deliberate mixed metaphor, Shakespeare reveals Hamlet's subconscious awareness that physical violence cannot resolve an existential crisis; taking arms against a sea merely guarantees that you will drown in the attempt.

`Klausur-Satz: Bei Aufgaben zu Shakespearean Tragedy: Hamlet's Soliloquy and the Tragic Flaw muss die theoriegeleitete Begruendung stets durch exakte Fachtermini und empirische Belege abgesichert werden.`

## Schritt 5 — ausprobieren: Duel of Tragic Drives: Violent Revenge vs. Existential Dread

VERGLEICH: Heroic Action vs. Intellectual Paralysis (选概念)

- Position A (The Feudal Hero / Laertes & Fortinbras):
  - Code: Blood vengeance, masculine honour, unhesitating physical combat.
  - Slogan: "To hell, allegiance! Vows, to the blackest devil! Conscience and grace, to the profoundest pit!" (Laertes).
  - Tragic fate: Swift action leading to mutual destruction and political collateral damage.
- Position B (The Renaissance Scholar / Prince Hamlet):
  - Code: Moral interrogation, verification of truth, fear of spiritual damnation.
  - Slogan: "Conscience does make cowards of us all."
  - Tragic fate: Prolonged delay (procrastination) that corrupts the entire Danish court and results in eight deaths instead of one.

Entscheidungsregel fuer die Klausur:
When analyzing Hamlet's delay in an exam, do not reduce him to a mere coward; explain how Shakespeare intentionally juxtaposes Hamlet with the foil characters Laertes (rash revenger) and Fortinbras (military conqueror) to highlight Hamlet's intellectual tragedy!

## Schritt 7 — check: Format-Check & Scoring

30 XP Schnelltest zur Verstaendigungssicherung:

FRAGE: What is the metrical definition of Blank Verse in Shakespearean drama?
ANTWORT: Unrhymed lines of ten syllables following an iambic rhythm (five alternating unstressed and stressed syllables).

FRAGE: Why does Hamlet hesitate to kill Claudius when he finds him alone on his knees in Act 3, Scene 3?
ANTWORT: Because Claudius is praying; Hamlet fears that killing him during prayer would send Claudius's soul straight to heaven instead of to hell, which would not be adequate revenge for his father's purgatorial torment.
