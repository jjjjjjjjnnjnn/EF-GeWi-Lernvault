### Hook / Phaenomen

For more than a century, European literature portrayed the African continent almost exclusively as a dark, brooding, primeval void without history, literature, or civilization. In Joseph Conrad's celebrated masterpiece "Heart of Darkness", Africans appeared merely as howling shadows on the riverbank, prehistoric savages without articulate language who produced only incomprehensible grunts. But in 1958, a young Nigerian author named Chinua Achebe picked up his pen and ignited a global literary revolution. He wrote "Things Fall Apart" – a breathtaking, complex epic about the pre-colonial Igbo civilization in eastern Nigeria. Achebe did not paint an idealized, romanticized fairytale: he depicted a sophisticated society with parliamentary village democracies, nuanced judicial councils, rich oral poetry, and profound agricultural rituals, but also with harsh taboos and human sacrifices. When white Christian missionaries and British colonial administrators arrive, the tragedy unfolds not because the Africans are primitive, but because two completely different worldviews collide head-on, tearing the ancient social fabric apart.

`Klausur-Satz: Chinua Achebe deconstructs Eurocentric colonial myths by presenting the pre-colonial Igbo community as a structurally intact, linguistically sophisticated society whose tragic disintegration stems from internal rigidity and external imperial violence.`

## Schritt 2 — entdecken: The Postcolonial Theoretical Glossary
PRETRAINING术语盒（核心4词，先读三遍中英，合书自测中文→英语）：

- 欧洲中心主义 — Eurocentrism: 将欧洲历史、价值观与文明形式预设为全世界衡量进步与真理的唯一普遍标准的意识形态偏见。 The worldview and intellectual practice of interpreting non-European cultures exclusively through the lens of European standards, values, and experiences.
- 文化碰撞 — Cultural Collision: 当两种具有完全不同宇宙观、宗教信仰与权力结构的文明发生剧烈接触甚至霸权征服时产生的破坏性震荡。 The violent shock and destabilization that occurs when two divergent social, religious, and legal systems clash during imperial conquest.
- 他者化 — Othering: 殖民霸权将非西方土著居民塑造为非理性、野蛮、落后或危险的“异类”，从而论证自身殖民统治合法性的修辞与心理机制。 The ideological process of defining a subordinate group as fundamentally foreign, inferior, or uncivilized to justify colonial dominance.
- 双重视野 / 杂糅性 — Double Perspective & Hybridity: 殖民地作家既深刻精通殖民者语言与西方文学经典，又深植于本土古老传统文化的双重视角，促成新文化认同的诞生。 The creative state of inhabiting both native and colonizer cultural traditions, transforming the English language into an authentic medium of African self-expression.

## Schritt 4 — ausprobieren: Close Reading Workshop on Achebe's Dual Lens

[Werkzeug: highlighter]

AUFGABE (analyze & explain, AFB I/II):
Examine the final paragraph of "Things Fall Apart":
"The Commissioner went away, taking three or four of the soldiers with him. In the many years he had toiled to bring civilization to different parts of Africa he had learnt a number of things. [...] He had already chosen the title of the book, after much thought: The Pacification of the Primitive Tribes of the Lower Niger."
1. Analyze the ironical contrast between the Commissioner's book title and the complex reality of Igbo life depicted by Achebe in the preceding twenty-four chapters.
2. Explain how Achebe uses the English language (African proverbs, syntax, rhythmic oral storytelling) to appropriate the language of the colonizer ("The Empire writes back").

MUSTERLOESUNG:
1. Ironical contrast of the book title:
   - The Commissioner's chosen title "The Pacification of the Primitive Tribes of the Lower Niger" reveals the absolute blindness of imperial ideology:
     - The word "Pacification" (bringing peace) is a grotesque euphemism for violent conquest, hanging of village elders, and the destruction of a functioning communal order.
     - The word "Primitive" reduces an intricate culture with philosophical proverbs, legal assemblies, and complex kinship networks to the level of illiterate savages.
   - By placing this bureaucratic, arrogant colonial perspective at the very end, Achebe exposes to the reader how history has been systematically distorted by European conquerors who wrote the history books while silencing the conquered.
2. Appropriation of the English language:
   - Achebe does not write in Queen's English of Victorian London; he invents a new, Africanized English: "Proverbs are the palm-oil with which words are eaten."
   - He seamlessly incorporates Igbo vocabulary (Chi, Obi, Egwugwu, Ogbanje) without italicizing or translating them condescendingly.
   - He adopts the cadence of traditional oral storytelling, thereby forcing the Western reader to step inside the African linguistic universe and prove that English can carry the weight of African thought.

## Schritt 5 — ausprobieren: Duel of Worldviews: Indigenous Tradition vs. Colonial Authority

VERGLEICH: Traditional Communal Balance vs. Colonial Imposition (选概念)

- Position A (Okonkwo's Hyper-Traditionalism):
  - Values: Absolute masculinity, military prowess, unyielding adherence to ancestral laws.
  - Fatal Flaw: Inability to adapt to historical change; mistakes empathy for cowardice and alienates his own family.
- Position B (The British Colonial Missionaries & Administrators):
  - Values: Monotheistic Christian salvation, British statutory law, bureaucratic governance.
  - Fatal Flaw: Cultural chauvinism; treats thousands of years of indigenous social harmony as non-existent barbarism.

Entscheidungsregel fuer die Klausur:
When analyzing postcolonial texts in an English exam, never present the pre-colonial culture as a flawless utopia; show that Achebe is a truthful realist who depicts both the noble strength AND the fatal rigidities of the Igbo society!

## Schritt 7 — check: Format-Check & Scoring

30 XP Schnelltest zur Verstaendigungssicherung:

FRAGE: What is the literary origin of the novel's title "Things Fall Apart"?
ANTWORT: It is taken from William Butler Yeats's famous poem "The Second Coming" ("Things fall apart; the centre cannot hold; Mere anarchy is loosed upon the world"), symbolizing the catastrophic collapse of an entire civilization.

FRAGE: Why does Okonkwo commit suicide at the end of the novel, and why is his suicide a final tragedy in Igbo culture?
ANTWORT: He realizes that his people will not fight a war against the British; furthermore, in Igbo religion suicide is an abomination (an offence against Ani, the earth goddess), meaning his own clansmen cannot bury his body and must treat him as an outcast.
