## Schritt 1 — entdecken

OBJECTIVES (after 20 minutes you can):

1. Name fifteen standard speech devices (Stilmittel) and mark at least five in an invented speech passage with their persuasive purpose.
2. Write a complete speech-analysis paragraph as a P.E.E. chain (Point, Evidence, Explanation) closing the loop of claim, quote and effect.
3. Evaluate (evaluate) the persuasiveness of a 100-word invented speech from one angle of ethos, pathos or logos with a Klausur-level closing sentence (AFB II-III).

Klausur-Satz: `The speaker employs stylistic devices to shape the audience's emotions and to strengthen the persuasiveness of the central argument.`

## Schritt 2 — entdecken

PRETRAINING (five core terms; read, cover, reproduce actively):

- Stylistic device (Stilmittel): a deliberate language choice (for example repetition or metaphor) creating a defined audience effect.
- Tricolon (Dreiklang): three parallel phrases or clauses, often building to a climax.
- Anaphora (Anapher): repetition of the same word or phrase at the start of successive clauses.
- Ethos, pathos, logos (Glaubwuerdigkeit, Gefuehl, Logik): credibility of the speaker, emotional appeal, logical argument.
- P.E.E. chain (P.E.E.-Kette): paragraph structure stating a point, quoting brief evidence and explaining its effect.

Klausur-Satz: `Each device can be analysed with the P.E.E. chain: a clear point, brief textual evidence, and an explanation of its effect on the audience.`

## Schritt 3 — entdecken

DISCOVERY (one concept plus diagram):

Speech analysis asks why a device works, not only which one appears. Formula: device plus position plus effect. Repeated openings (anaphora) drum up emotion; repeated endings gather echoes; tricolon accelerates feeling; rhetorical questions pull listeners into dialogue; antithesis builds "we vs they" tension. Every passage faces three questions: what should listeners feel (pathos), what should they believe (logos), why should they trust the speaker (ethos). Fifteen devices serve exactly these questions.

```diagram
         SPEECH EFFECT = DEVICE + POSITION + PURPOSE
           +------------------+------------------+
           |  SOUND           |  MEANING         |
           |  alliteration    |  metaphor/simile |
           |  anaphora        |  hyperbole       |
           |  epiphora        |  personification |
           |  tricolon        |  emotive language|
           |  parallelism     |  antithesis      |
           +------------------+------------------+
           |  CONTACT         |  ARGUMENT        |
           |  rhetorical ?    |  inclusive "we"  |
           |  direct address  |  imperative      |
           |  repetition      |  (ethos/pathos/  |
           |                  |   logos)         |
           +------------------+------------------+
      P.E.E.: Point -> "quote" -> Effect on audience
```

Klausur-Satz: `The effect of a device depends on its position in the speech and on the speaker's persuasive purpose.`

## Anekdote & Fun-Fact

**Anekdote / Fun-Fact (EN)**: Speech coaches count beats, not just words. A tricolon works like three drum hits: the first states, the second strengthens, the third lands. That is why invented campaign lines so often come in threes — the brain remembers the third hit best.

**Bezug zum Konzept**: `Rhythm supports rhetoric: patterns of three guide attention and make the climax memorable.`

## Schritt 4 — ausprobieren

MODEL TASK (worked example with solution):

[Werkzeug: highlighter]

TASK (analyze, AFB II): Read the original 100-word model speech below (in the style of a civil-rights or wartime address, fully invented, no quotation from real speeches). Highlight 5 devices and analyse two of them in a P.E.E. paragraph.

Model speech "The Bridge We Build" (100 words, original): "We did not come here to wait. We came to walk, to work, to win. Will our children ask whether we stood aside? We will answer: we stood together. They tell us the river is too wide; I tell you we are the bridge. We, the patient, we, the brave, we, the many — can we fail if we move as one? Stand up, speak out, hold on. The night is dark, but our purpose is dawn. Let history record not our fear, but our first step."

HELP:
1. Mark sound patterns — tricolon ("to walk, to work, to win"), anaphora ("We ... We ..."), alliteration ("stood aside and stood together").
2. Mark contact devices — rhetorical questions ("Will our children ask ...?", "can we fail ...?"), imperatives ("Stand up, speak out, hold on"), inclusive "we".
3. Take two finds and build one P.E.E. chain each: Point (device plus purpose), Evidence (eight words maximum), Explanation (effect on pathos or logos).

MODEL SOLUTION: Point: The tricolon "to walk, to work, to win" accelerates the rhythm. Evidence: three parallel infinitives in one line. Explanation: The rising beat builds collective energy (pathos) and presents action as inevitable, which strengthens the leadership ethos. Point 2: The rhetorical question "can we fail if we move as one?" involves the audience. Evidence: a question with no answer expected. Explanation: Listeners silently answer "no", so they persuade themselves; this supports logos by turning unity into the logical condition of success. Inventory of all 15 devices in the speech: 1 tricolon, 2 anaphora, 3 echo (wait and win), 4 parallelism, 5 rhetorical question (twice), 6 hyperbole ("we are the bridge"), 7 metaphor ("purpose is dawn"), 8 antithesis (night vs dawn), 9 personification ("history record"), 10 alliteration, 11 antithesis (too wide vs bridge; fear vs first step), 12 repetition ("we"), 13 emotive language (brave, dark, dawn), 14 inclusive pronoun "we", 15 imperative ("Stand up ...").

Klausur-Satz: `By combining tricolon and rhetorical questions, the speaker turns passive listeners into active supporters of the cause.`

## Schritt 5 — ausprobieren

COMPARISON (two procedures):

COMPARISON: Decide first — (i) analysis procedure (analyze: name the device plus quote plus effect, answering how persuasion works) or (ii) evaluation procedure (evaluate: judge persuasiveness with balance and verdict, answering how effective and how limited) — then write.

TASK A: Analyze how the speaker uses inclusive language in "we, the patient, we, the brave, we, the many".
TASK B: Evaluate whether the speech is convincing for a present-day school audience.

HELP: A asks "how" plus one device -> procedure (i), P.E.E. chain. B asks "whether" and "convincing" -> procedure (ii), verdict plus balance. Rule: "how" plus a device name requires analysis; "how convincing", "evaluate" and "to what extent" require evaluation with admitted strength, stated limit and verdict.

ANSWER: A requires procedure (i): Point — inclusive anaphora builds unity; Evidence — "we, the patient, we, the brave"; Explanation — repeated "we" erases distance between speaker and audience and creates collective pathos. B requires procedure (ii): The speech is largely convincing because rhythm and direct address create urgency; its limit is that metaphors ("we are the bridge") inspire but offer no concrete policy (logos gap). Overall, it moves the audience emotionally more than it convinces logically.

Klausur-Satz: `While the inclusive language effectively creates solidarity, its persuasive power remains mainly emotional rather than logical.`

## Schritt 6 — check

CHECK (three questions with answers):

FRAGE: What are the three steps of a P.E.E. paragraph in speech analysis? | ANTWORT: Point (device plus purpose), Evidence (short quote, eight words maximum), Explanation (audience effect in ethos, pathos or logos terms).
FRAGE: What distinguishes anaphora from tricolon? | ANTWORT: Anaphora repeats the same opening across clauses ("We came ... We will ..."); tricolon groups three parallel elements ("walk, work, win") — both create rhythm, but anaphora binds while tricolon climaxes.
FRAGE: Name one device per appeal (ethos, pathos, logos). | ANTWORT: Ethos — inclusive "we" and confident imperatives signal leadership; pathos — emotive language and metaphor stir feelings; logos — antithesis and rhetorical questions frame unity as the logical choice.

Klausur-Satz: `A complete analysis always connects the quoted device to its concrete effect on the audience.`

## Fehlvorstellung

(not a Schritt; parser skips automatically)

1. Error: "Listing device names completes the analysis."
   Correction: Bare labels stay on AFB I. Every device needs an effect sentence stating what listeners feel or believe. P.E.E. without Explanation is unfinished.
   Korrektur-Satz: `Naming a device without explaining its effect on the audience does not constitute an analysis.`

2. Error: "Rhetorical questions expect an answer."
   Correction: Their force lies in needing none. Listeners supply the intended answer silently and thereby persuade themselves. Name the expected answer in the analysis.
   Korrektur-Satz: `A rhetorical question is effective precisely because the audience supplies the intended answer silently.`

## Schritt 8 — entdecken

TAKEAWAY (core summary):

Devices are tools; effects score. Mark three classes: sound (alliteration, anaphora, tricolon), meaning (metaphor, hyperbole, antithesis), contact (rhetorical question, address, imperative). Analyse each find in three P.E.E. sentences: name the device, quote half a line, state the pathos, logos or ethos effect. For evaluation, praise then limit then judge: strong feeling and weak logic is a universal starting point.
Takeaway-Satz: `Devices create rhythm, images and contact; analysis proves their effect, evaluation weighs their limits.`
